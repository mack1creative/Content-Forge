# Chapter 1: Prompt Engineering for Clients

Look, I'm going to be honest with you. Most businesses are terrible at using AI. They type some vague nonsense into ChatGPT, get garbage back, and then conclude that AI "isn't that useful."

That's your opportunity.

Companies are spending money on AI tools but getting almost nothing out of them. They need someone who actually knows how to write prompts that work — and they'll pay you for it.

Here's the thing: the prompts aren't the hard part. Anyone can learn this. What matters is understanding what the client actually needs and crafting prompts that deliver it. It's part technical, part creative, and honestly, mostly just giving a damn about the result.

## How This Works

The gap between what AI can do and what people get from it is massive. Someone spends 30 seconds typing "write me something good" and wonders why they got nothing useful. Meanwhile, someone who understands prompting can get production-ready output in minutes.

The models haven't changed — the prompts have. That's the whole game right there.

## Getting Started

### Week 1-2: Actually Learn This Stuff

Before you charge people, you need to actually know what you're doing. Here's the basics:

- **Be specific** — Vague prompts get vague results. Tell the AI exactly what you want.
- **Assign roles** — "Act as a [profession]" unlocks all kinds of knowledge.
- **Give structure** — Bullet points, headers, numbered lists = cleaner outputs.
- **Add context** — The more relevant background, the better the result.
- **Set limits** — Sometimes telling AI what NOT to do is more powerful than what to do.
- **Iterate** — The best prompts come from refining, not from getting it right the first time.

Practice by writing 10 different prompts for the same task. Compare the outputs. Figure out what actually made the difference.

### Week 3: Pick a Niche

Don't try to be everything to everyone. Pick one industry and own it.

Good niches to start with:

- Real estate — agents need property descriptions, emails, content
- E-commerce — product descriptions, ad copy, emails
- Coaches and consultants — course outlines, bio writing, content ideas
- Local businesses — plumbers, dentists, lawyers all need content but hate writing it

Pick something you actually know something about. It'll make everything easier.

### Weeks 4-6: Build Your Portfolio

Before you pitch anyone, have something to show them. Create 5-10 sample prompts specifically for your chosen niche. These are your proof.

For each sample, show:

1. The problem the prompt solves
2. The actual prompt (clean and formatted)
3. What the prompt produces
4. Why it works

Something like: a real estate agent needs property descriptions fast. You write a prompt that creates compelling 150-word descriptions with specific details. It works because you set clear constraints and gave it a role.

### Weeks 7-8: Find Clients

Here's what actually works:

**LinkedIn** — Post 3-5 times per week about AI tips in your niche. Comment on posts by business owners. Send DMs to people who engage with your content.

**Cold email** — Keep it simple:

"Hey [Name],

I help [industry] companies get way better results from ChatGPT by writing custom prompts for their specific workflow.

One client went from spending 2 hours on [task] to 15 minutes. Happy to show you how.

Book 15 minutes here: [calendar link]

No pressure."

**Upwork and Fiverr** — Create a clear service. Start with competitive pricing to get first reviews. Overdeliver on your first few orders.

### Ongoing: Deliver and Keep Clients

Your services:

- Prompt audit ($50-150) — Review their prompts, show improvements
- Prompt package ($100-500) — 5-10 custom prompts for their business
- Training session ($200-400) — Teach their team for an hour
- Monthly retainer ($500-2,000) — Keep their prompts optimized

The delivery process: kickoff call to understand their needs, research their industry, draft prompts, test them, deliver with instructions, and follow up a week later.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Being too general — Say "I write prompts for real estate agents" not "I write AI prompts"
- Not showing results — Clients care about outcomes, not prompts
- Undercharging — You're selling expertise, not time
- Skipping the niche — Specialize first
- Not following up — Check in after delivery, ask for testimonials

## 90-Day Plan

**Days 1-14:** Study prompt fundamentals (5+ hours), practice 20+ prompts, test across different AI tools.

**Days 15-30:** Create 5 sample prompts, build a simple portfolio site, set up booking and invoicing.

**Days 31-60:** Send 50 cold outreach messages, post 3x weekly on LinkedIn, apply to 5 freelance platforms, complete first paid project.

**Days 61-90:** Deliver first 5 projects with great reviews, ask for testimonials, raise prices 20%, pitch one retainer deal.

This is a skill you can build fast. The demand is real. And once you've got a few happy clients, they'll refer you to others. Most prompt engineers who stick with it for 6 months are making $2,000-5,000/month.

Just start. Don't wait until you feel ready.

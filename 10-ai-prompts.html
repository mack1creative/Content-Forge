10 AI Prompts That Actually Get Results

Unlock real output from ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini with these battle-tested prompts

1. The "Be Specific" Prompt

"I need a [type of content] for [target audience]. It should be [tone/voice]. Include [key points]. Keep it [length]. Write it now."
Why it works: AI guesses wrong when you're vague. This forces clarity.

2. The "Role Play" Prompt

"Act as a [profession/expert]. I have a [situation/problem]. Give me [advice/solution] as if you were explaining it to a beginner."
Why it works: Role-playing triggers domain-specific knowledge and terminology.

3. The "Expand vs. Condense" Prompt

"Take this [paragraph/idea]: [paste content]. Either expand it with more detail OR condense it to its core point — choose whichever serves the reader better."
Why it works: Gives AI permission to make judgment calls, producing better output.

4. The "Example Injection" Prompt

"Write a [type of content] about [topic]. Here's an example of the style I want: [paste example]. Match this tone, structure, and depth."
Why it works: Shows — doesn't just tell — AI what good looks like.

5. The "Negative Constraint" Prompt

"Write about [topic] but avoid: [list things to exclude]. Do NOT mention [forbidden topics]. Keep the focus on [required focus]."
Why it works: Tells AI what to leave out, which sharpens the output more than what to include.

6. The "Format Flip" Prompt

"Convert this data [paste data] into a [format of choice: email, tweet thread, bullet list, script]. Make it ready to use with no edits needed."
Why it works: Turns raw outputs into deployable content in one go.

7. The "Iterative Refinement" Prompt

"First, write a rough draft of [topic]. Then ask yourself: What's missing? What's unclear? Revise with those improvements."
Why it works: Forces AI to self-edit instead of dumping first-draft garbage.

8. The "Multiple Option" Prompt

"Give me 3 different approaches to [problem]. For each, provide the pros, cons, and one sentence on who it's best for."
Why it works: Breaks you out of "one right answer" thinking.

9. The "Chain of Thought" Prompt

"Before giving me the answer, show me your reasoning. Walk through how you'd approach this, what you'd consider, then give me your conclusion."
Why it works: Reveals the "why" behind the output — critical for catching errors or learning.

10. The "Apply It Now" Prompt

"After explaining [concept], give me one specific, actionable example I can try today. Keep it practical, not theoretical."
Why it works: Prevents "wall of text syndrome" — every prompt ends with implementation.
